# SY0-701 objective 5.1: Security governance

[Security+ index](../../README.md) · Domain 5: Security Program Management and Oversight

Original study notes aligned to the public CompTIA SY0-701 V7 blueprint; not official training or exam questions.

## Explain it

Governance establishes who sets direction, who owns decisions, and how conformance is checked. A policy states organizational intent, a standard defines mandatory specifics, a procedure describes repeatable steps, and a guideline offers recommended practice. Playbooks direct response for particular events. Boards and committees provide oversight; system/data owners make accountability decisions, while custodians or stewards operate or manage data under delegated responsibilities.

External law, regulation, contract and industry requirements shape policies but should be identified precisely rather than assumed universal. Governance covers acceptable use, access, physical security, encryption, development, change, incident response, continuity and disaster recovery. Review and revise documents when technology, threats or obligations change; publish them and retain evidence of acknowledgment or implementation.

## Deeper distinctions

Governance sets authority and accountability before choosing tools. A board or executive body sets risk appetite; policy owners define requirements; system/data owners accept operational accountability; custodians implement and maintain controls; independent assessors test them. Centralized governance improves consistency, while decentralized execution may fit varied business units if oversight remains clear.

A policy says what must be achieved, a standard defines mandatory measurable rules, a procedure defines steps, and a guideline recommends an approach. A playbook gives event-specific actions. Keep document version, approval, effective date, owner, review cadence and exception path. Map laws and contracts to scope before applying them; “regulated” does not mean every requirement applies to every system. Onboarding/offboarding, development, continuity and change management all need assigned roles.

**Governance drill:** A password standard requires MFA for privileged access. Identify policy, technical enforcement, exception owner and evidence an auditor would request.

## Additional blueprint concepts

The official scope includes data **controller**, **processor**, owner and custodian/steward roles. A controller determines purposes and means of personal-data processing; a processor acts on its behalf under applicable law and contract. A data owner is accountable for a data set within the organization, while a custodian operates safeguards. Do not equate these different role frameworks. Governance documents include acceptable use, SDLC, incident response, continuity, disaster recovery and change management; external requirements can be local, national or global. Boards, committees and government bodies play different oversight roles.

## Apply it

An ISSO sees a policy requiring quarterly access review but no assigned reviewer. Clarify accountable owner, procedure, evidence format, exception escalation and oversight reporting.

## Active recall

Distinguish a policy, standard, procedure and guideline with one access-control example each.
