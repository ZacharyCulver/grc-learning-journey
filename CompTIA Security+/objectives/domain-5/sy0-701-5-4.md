# SY0-701 objective 5.4: Security compliance

[Security+ index](../../README.md) · Domain 5: Security Program Management and Oversight

Original study notes aligned to the public CompTIA SY0-701 V7 blueprint; not official training or exam questions.

## Explain it

Compliance demonstrates adherence to applicable obligations, not immunity from attacks. Identify legal, regulatory, contractual and internal requirements; map each to controls, owners, evidence and review cadence. Track exceptions and remediation, preserve evidence with appropriate integrity and retention, and report gaps honestly. Privacy obligations require attention to collection, processing, retention, disclosure and data subject rights where applicable.

A control can satisfy several obligations, but differences in scope and timing matter. Conduct gap analysis when requirements change or systems are added. Technical safeguards and documentation should tell the same story: an access policy without configuration or review evidence is incomplete.

## Deeper distinctions

Compliance begins with an obligations inventory: requirement, applicable system/data, accountable owner, control, evidence, testing interval and exception process. Requirements may arise from regulation, contract or internal policy; an audit finding can reveal failure but does not create the underlying obligation. A control may map to multiple obligations, yet evidence must match each scope and timeframe.

Distinguish design (the control is appropriately specified) from operation (it worked during the period). A configuration screenshot on one day may not prove a quarterly access review occurred. Privacy work also concerns collection purpose, minimization, use, sharing, retention and disposal under applicable rules. Report gaps and remediation dates; avoid treating a compliance checkbox as a guarantee of security.

**Evidence drill:** For a 24-hour deprovisioning requirement, define population, sample, timestamps, exceptions and test result. Explain what you would do if HR and identity clocks disagree.

## Additional blueprint concepts

Compliance reporting can be internal or external, and failure can cause fines, sanctions, license loss, contract consequences and reputational harm. Due diligence is investigation before a decision; due care is ongoing prudent action. An attestation states or confirms conformity, but it should be supported by monitoring and evidence. Automation helps collect evidence consistently, yet it can repeat a wrong interpretation at scale.

Privacy analysis should identify data inventory, controller/processor responsibilities, ownership and retention. Rights such as erasure or “right to be forgotten” depend on applicable law and exceptions; they are not universal commands to delete every record immediately. Record jurisdiction and legal basis before stating a requirement.

## Apply it

A policy says terminated users lose access within a defined window. Sample HR events, identity deprovisioning timestamps and exception tickets to test actual performance; assign remediation for misses.

## Active recall

Describe what evidence would support an encryption requirement and what a compliance report should say when a control failed.
