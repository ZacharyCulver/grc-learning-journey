# SY0-701 objective 2.4: Indicators and malicious activity

[Security+ index](../../README.md) · Domain 2: Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Mitigations

Original study notes aligned to the public CompTIA SY0-701 V7 blueprint; not official training or exam questions.

## Explain it

Malware classes describe behavior: ransomware encrypts or blocks access for extortion; a worm self-propagates; a trojan masquerades as benign; spyware gathers information; a rootkit hides privileged persistence. Attacks may also involve DDoS, DNS manipulation, on-path interception, wireless abuse, password spraying, brute force, replay and application injection. Password spraying tries a few common passwords against many accounts, often to avoid lockout; brute force exhaustively guesses one target.

Indicators include unusual account lockouts, impossible or concurrent sessions, unexpected outbound traffic, blocked content, altered files and abnormal resource use. An indicator is a lead, not proof. Correlate timestamps, identity, endpoint, network and application logs; preserve evidence and avoid premature attribution.

## Deeper distinctions

Separate the **attack technique** from the **observable indicator**. A cluster of failed logins across many accounts may suggest spraying; one account with rapid attempts may suggest brute force; a successful login from an unusual location warrants correlation, not automatic proof of compromise. Replay reuses a captured valid exchange, while an on-path attacker intercepts or manipulates live traffic. DNS poisoning or hijacking changes where a name resolves; a DDoS seeks to exhaust service capacity.

Malware categories overlap. Ransomware may also steal data; a trojan can deliver spyware or a rootkit; a worm spreads without requiring each victim to launch it. Process behavior, persistence, file changes, network connections and identity events together build the story. Preserve timestamps and source records before containment actions erase useful artifacts.

**Decision drill:** An endpoint shows encryption activity, a new scheduled task and outbound transfers. Describe likely malicious behavior, immediate containment, evidence to preserve and why restoring files alone would miss the exfiltration risk.

## Additional blueprint concepts

A logic bomb triggers when a condition is met; bloatware is unwanted software that may increase exposure without being classic malware. Physical brute force forces entry; RFID cloning copies badge data where the technology permits it. Reflected and amplified DDoS routes responses from third parties toward a victim; amplification makes the response larger than the request.

A cryptographic downgrade coerces a weaker protocol or setting; a collision finds two inputs with the same digest, while a birthday attack exploits collision probability. These are not interchangeable with password spraying. “Impossible travel,” missing logs, unusual out-of-cycle logging and newly inaccessible resources are investigation cues; confirm with clock, VPN, session and business context.

## Apply it

Several accounts each show one failed login from the same source followed by one success. Investigate password spraying and the successful session, reset affected credentials, and check MFA and source context.

## Active recall

How would you distinguish a worm from a trojan, spraying from brute force, and a suspicious signal from a confirmed incident?
