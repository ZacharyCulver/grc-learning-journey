# SY0-701 objective 3.3: Protecting data

[Security+ index](../../README.md) · Domain 3: Security Architecture

Original study notes aligned to the public CompTIA SY0-701 V7 blueprint; not official training or exam questions.

## Explain it

Classify information by sensitivity, legal obligations and business criticality. Types include personal, regulated, financial, intellectual-property, trade-secret and critical operational data. Data may be at rest, in transit or in use; protection must match its state and handling path. Encryption protects confidentiality when keys are managed correctly; hashing helps detect change; masking limits display; tokenization substitutes a sensitive value; permission restrictions limit use; segmentation limits reach.

Retention, geographic location and data sovereignty can change where and how information may be stored or processed. A label alone is not protection: establish ownership, permitted use, access review, transfer controls, backup and disposal requirements. Ensure logs and test data do not leak the same sensitive values you protected in production.

## Deeper distinctions

Start with **what the data is**, **where it moves**, **who owns it**, and **which rule applies**. Public, internal, confidential and restricted are labels whose definitions must be set by the organization. Personal, financial, legal, trade-secret and regulated information may have overlapping obligations. Data sovereignty and geographic restrictions influence provider and backup-region selection.

At rest, use access controls and encryption with protected keys; in transit, authenticate endpoints and use appropriate transport encryption; in use, minimize exposure in memory, displays, exports and logs. A hash detects change under the right trust model but does not encrypt; masking hides displayed portions but may leave stored data intact; tokenization reduces the spread of original sensitive values if mapping is separately protected. DLP and permission reviews can reinforce handling policy.

**Data-flow drill:** Follow a customer record from browser to API, database, backup and support dashboard. At each stop identify state, reader, retention, applicable control and evidence that it works.

## Additional blueprint concepts

Human-readable data (a report or spreadsheet) and non-human-readable data (binary or encoded records) can carry the same sensitivity. Classification follows content and obligations, not file extension. Geolocation is where data or processing actually occurs; sovereignty can impose legal requirements based on jurisdiction. Verify primary storage, replicas, logs and backups before claiming geographic restriction. A data-flow map should include service accounts and machine-to-machine transfers, not just human viewers.

## Apply it

A team copies production records into a test environment. Require approved purpose, minimum data, de-identification or tokenization, restricted access and retention limits; verify that backups and logs obey the same rules.

## Active recall

Choose a protection for a data set at rest, moving between services, and shown to support staff; explain what each does not solve.
