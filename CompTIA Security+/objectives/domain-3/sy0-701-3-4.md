# SY0-701 objective 3.4: Resilience and recovery

[Security+ index](../../README.md) · Domain 3: Security Architecture

Original study notes aligned to the public CompTIA SY0-701 V7 blueprint; not official training or exam questions.

## Explain it

High availability keeps service running through component failure with redundancy, failover, clustering or load balancing. Backup protects recoverability after deletion, corruption or ransomware; it is not the same as live redundancy, which can replicate errors. Set recovery time objective (RTO) for acceptable downtime and recovery point objective (RPO) for tolerable data loss, then align backup frequency and architecture to those needs.

Hot, warm and cold sites trade cost against readiness. Geographically separated systems can reduce common-cause outages but create latency and coordination challenges. Encrypt and protect backups, keep an appropriate offline or immutable copy, and test actual restoration. Tabletop exercises examine decisions; simulations and failover tests exercise procedures or technology. Record results and correct gaps.

## Deeper distinctions

Availability architecture reduces failure impact; recoverability restores service and data after a bad event. Clusters and load balancers can shift traffic when a node fails, but shared DNS, storage, power or identity can still be single points of failure. Geographic dispersion reduces some common-cause risks. Capacity planning must account for degraded mode: can the surviving nodes carry the whole workload?

Recovery objectives should be measured in exercises, not inferred from product labels. RPO limits acceptable lost data; RTO limits downtime. A warm site may be quicker than a cold site but needs current data, configuration and tested failover. Backups should be scheduled, encrypted, access-controlled, protected from ransomware and restored in tests. A snapshot is convenient but might share the same account or failure domain; verify retention and isolation. UPS and generators handle different power-duration needs.

**Decision drill:** Two replicas share one storage array. Describe what happens under node failure, corrupted data, storage failure and ransomware. Select a separate safeguard for each failure.

## Apply it

A mission system needs an RPO of one hour and RTO of four hours. Assess whether daily backups can meet the data-loss objective, then test restoration and failover against both targets.

## Active recall

Explain why replication alone is not a backup. What evidence would prove that a recovery plan works?
