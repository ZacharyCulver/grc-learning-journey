# SY0-701 objective 4.6: Identity and access management

[Security+ index](../../README.md) · Domain 4: Security Operations

Original study notes aligned to the public CompTIA SY0-701 V7 blueprint; not official training or exam questions.

## Explain it

Provision access from an approved role and remove it promptly on departure or role change. Identification names a principal, authentication proves it, and authorization determines permitted actions. MFA combines independent factor categories, not two passwords. SSO reduces repeated sign-ins, while federation passes identity assertions across trust boundaries. Privileged access should be separate, time-bounded where possible, reviewed and logged.

Compare discretionary, mandatory, role-based and attribute-based access decisions. Least privilege limits rights; separation of duties prevents one person controlling a sensitive process end to end. Review service accounts and secrets as well as people; account lifecycle failures can undermine otherwise strong login controls.

## Deeper distinctions

The account lifecycle is request, approve, provision, modify, review and revoke. Joiner/mover/leaver failures cause privilege creep. Authentication methods include passwords, possession factors and biometrics; phishing-resistant methods bind authentication to a legitimate service more robustly than a reusable one-time code. MFA requires different factor classes. SSO centralizes sign-in experience; federation exchanges identity assertions between organizations; neither automatically guarantees least privilege.

DAC lets owners control permissions, MAC applies centrally enforced labels, RBAC assigns rights by role, and ABAC evaluates attributes such as department, resource and context. Use least privilege and separation of duties for sensitive workflows; PAM can add check-out, approval and session recording for administrator access. Service identities need inventory, scoped permissions, secret rotation and noninteractive handling. Deprovisioning must include tokens and active sessions when risk demands.

**Access drill:** A user changes jobs and retains old cloud roles. Identify the authoritative role source, approval, entitlement diff, review evidence and urgent removal path.

## Additional blueprint concepts

Identity proofing establishes who a person is before issuing credentials; authentication checks a later claim. Attestation is a periodic confirmation that granted access remains appropriate. Rule-based controls evaluate explicit conditions, such as time-of-day or source; attribute-based decisions can combine user, resource and environmental attributes. LDAP queries directory information, SAML carries federation assertions, and OAuth delegates authorization to an application; OAuth alone is not an identity proofing or authentication protocol.

Password guidance should weigh length, reuse, manager support, compromise checks and organizational policy; forced frequent expiration without a reason can encourage poor behavior. Passwordless methods still need secure enrollment and recovery. Factor examples include something known, possessed, inherent, or location-based, with caveats about spoofing and independence. PAM may provide vaulting, just-in-time rights and short-lived credentials. Verify revocation of active tokens/sessions after account removal.

## Apply it

A contractor changes teams but retains former administrator permissions. Reconcile current role with entitlements, remove excess rights, review recent activity and improve transfer/offboarding workflows.

## Active recall

Explain authentication versus authorization, RBAC versus ABAC, and why two knowledge factors are not true MFA.
