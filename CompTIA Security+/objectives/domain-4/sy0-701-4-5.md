# SY0-701 objective 4.5: Enhancing enterprise security capabilities

[Security+ index](../../README.md) · Domain 4: Security Operations

Original study notes aligned to the public CompTIA SY0-701 V7 blueprint; not official training or exam questions.

## Explain it

Firewalls and access lists constrain traffic; screened subnets isolate public services; NAC checks device admission. IDS alerts and IPS can block, while EDR investigates and responds at endpoints and XDR correlates across sources. DLP monitors or restricts sensitive data movement; file-integrity monitoring reveals unauthorized changes; web and DNS filtering block risky destinations. Secure protocol selection includes the port, transport and configuration, not just a familiar acronym.

Email authentication uses SPF to authorize senders, DKIM to sign message content and DMARC to publish receiver handling/reporting based on alignment. None alone guarantees that a message is harmless. Group Policy or SELinux can enforce operating-system security; user-behavior analytics highlights anomalies but requires investigation.

## Deeper distinctions

Match a capability to an observable/control point. A firewall or ACL restricts routes and ports; a WAF examines web-layer requests; IDS signals suspicious traffic; IPS can block it; EDR responds on hosts; DLP addresses sensitive data movement. A screened subnet limits direct access to internal systems. NAC governs device admission. File-integrity monitoring identifies unauthorized changes but needs a trusted baseline and triage process.

Secure protocols require identity validation and current configuration. Web and DNS filtering reduce risky destinations but cannot make every allowed site safe. SPF checks authorized sending hosts, DKIM checks a domain's cryptographic signature, and DMARC uses alignment and policy for receivers; they do not prove the human sender's intent. Group Policy and SELinux enforce OS rules at different layers; UBA identifies unusual patterns but does not alone establish maliciousness.

**Control-selection drill:** For outbound exfiltration, compare DLP, DNS filtering, egress firewall policy and EDR. Which sees the data, destination or endpoint behavior, and what could each miss?

## Apply it

A phishing campaign uses a spoofed domain and malicious URL. Combine SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment with URL filtering, endpoint telemetry and user reporting; verify each control's limits.

## Active recall

Compare IDS, IPS and EDR; explain the distinct purposes of SPF, DKIM and DMARC.
