# SY0-701 objective 4.3: Vulnerability management

[Security+ index](../../README.md) · Domain 4: Security Operations

Original study notes aligned to the public CompTIA SY0-701 V7 blueprint; not official training or exam questions.

## Explain it

The cycle is discover, confirm, contextualize, prioritize, remediate or mitigate, validate and report. Methods include authenticated scans, application static/dynamic analysis, dependency monitoring, threat intelligence, penetration tests and disclosure channels. CVE identifies a publicly cataloged flaw; CVSS estimates technical severity, not the organization's full business risk. False positives need confirmation and false negatives mean a clean scan is not proof of safety.

Prioritize by exploitability, exposure, asset importance, compensating controls and risk appetite. Responses include patches, configuration fixes, segmentation, temporary restrictions, documented exceptions and, ultimately, retirement. Rescan or otherwise verify the fix; track owner, due date and residual risk in a finding or POA&M.

## Deeper distinctions

Scanning is discovery, not a complete risk decision. An authenticated scan can see configuration and patch state more deeply than an unauthenticated one. Static analysis examines code without running it; dynamic analysis exercises behavior. Penetration testing validates exploit paths within agreed rules. Threat feeds and disclosure programs can surface new exposure between scans. CVE names a known vulnerability; CVSS is a severity framework; neither by itself sets your remediation deadline.

Confirm a finding and its affected version, then consider reachability, exploit activity, privilege required, asset value, impact and existing controls. A false positive wastes work if unverified, while a false negative means there may be unseen risk. Treatment can be patch, configuration change, segmentation, compensating control or approved temporary exception. Retesting should show the specific weakness is absent, not just that a ticket was closed. A POA&M or similar register captures owner, deadline, action and residual risk.

**Triage drill:** Rank an exploited medium-severity internet-facing flaw against a critical-severity lab flaw with no route. State your assumptions and what new fact could reverse the ordering.

## Additional blueprint concepts

Exposure factor is the fraction of value a loss event may affect; in vulnerability triage it is only one business-impact input, not a CVSS component. Environmental context and risk tolerance can change priority. Threat intelligence may come from OSINT, commercial feeds, information-sharing groups or even dark-web reporting; validate quality and relevance. A responsible-disclosure or bug-bounty channel is an identification source, not permission for unlimited testing.

Insurance may transfer some financial loss but does not remediate the vulnerability. An exception or exemption requires defined scope, approval, expiration/review and residual-risk ownership. Validate a fix through rescan, audit or targeted verification and report what remains open.

## Apply it

A critical CVSS finding affects an isolated lab host while a lower-scored internet-facing flaw is actively exploited. Context may justify fixing the exposed system first; document the decision and retest both.
