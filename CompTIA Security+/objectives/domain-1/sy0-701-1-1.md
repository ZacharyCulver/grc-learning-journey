# SY0-701 objective 1.1: Security control types and categories

[Security+ index](../../README.md) · Domain 1: General Security Concepts

These are original study notes aligned to the public CompTIA SY0-701 V7 exam objectives, not official CompTIA training or exam questions.

## Explain it

A control's **implementation category** describes how it is delivered: technical (firewall rule), managerial (risk assessment or policy), operational (a practiced backup procedure), or physical (door lock). Its **function** describes what it does: preventive blocks, deterrent discourages, detective discovers, corrective restores, compensating substitutes when the preferred safeguard is impractical, and directive tells people what to do. One safeguard can occupy multiple labels: a camera is physical and generally detective; a locked door is physical and preventive. Do not confuse a written policy (directive/managerial) with evidence that staff actually follow it (operational).

In a scenario, identify the threat and the moment the control acts. An alert after an unauthorized login is detective, even if the team later uses it to correct the incident. A firewall rule preventing access is preventive, not detective merely because it logs traffic. Compensating controls need to address the original risk and should be documented and approved.

## Deeper distinctions

A useful two-axis test is **how it is implemented** versus **when it affects the event**. A badge reader is technical/physical at a door and preventive when it denies entry; its access log is detective. A staffed checkpoint is operational and can deter, prevent, and detect. A risk assessment is managerial and informs control selection rather than directly stopping a packet.

Directive controls shape behavior through instructions; a standard or sign must be communicated and enforced to matter. Corrective controls restore a desired state after harm, whereas a compensating control is an alternative safeguard for an unmet requirement. Compensation is not a synonym for “backup” or “weak but better than nothing.” Evidence of control effectiveness could be a configured rule plus a test showing prohibited access fails, or a restoration record showing the backup actually worked.

**Decision drill:** A locked rack, weekly log review, and documented exception for an unpatchable device address different stages. Identify each control's implementation and function, then name the residual risk that remains.

## Apply it

A legacy application cannot support MFA. Record the gap, restrict its network reach, require a controlled jump host and heightened monitoring, and document the residual risk. The combination may compensate but is not automatically equivalent to MFA.

## Active recall

Classify a security guard, log review, policy, and backup restoration by implementation and function. Explain why more than one label may apply.
