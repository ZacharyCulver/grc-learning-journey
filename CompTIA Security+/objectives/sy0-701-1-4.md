# SY0-701 objective 1.4: Cryptographic solutions

[Security+ index](../README.md) · Domain 1: General Security Concepts

These are original study notes aligned to the public CompTIA SY0-701 V7 exam objectives, not official CompTIA training or exam questions.

## Explain it

Symmetric cryptography uses a shared secret and is efficient for bulk data; asymmetric cryptography uses a public/private pair for key establishment and signatures. A hybrid protocol can authenticate or establish a session with asymmetric methods, then protect traffic using a symmetric session key. Hashing produces a one-way digest for integrity; passwords require unique salts and a purpose-built slow password-hashing or key-derivation method rather than plain fast hashes. Digital signatures bind an identity to content and can support integrity, authenticity, and non-repudiation; they do not by themselves hide the message.

A PKI certificate binds a public key to an identity under a certificate authority. Certificate validation checks trust chain, identity, validity, and revocation status (CRL or OCSP), subject to the protocol's behavior. A CSR requests issuance; private keys must remain protected. TPMs and HSMs safeguard key operations in different form factors; key escrow and KMS address controlled recovery/management. Encryption may cover full disk, file, database, or traffic; tokenization replaces sensitive values, masking obscures display, and steganography hides existence rather than securing contents. Blockchain is a tamper-evident ledger pattern, not a universal confidentiality solution.

## Apply it

A database contains payment identifiers. Compare encryption at rest, field-level tokenization, display masking, and TLS in transit; select controls for distinct exposure paths and document key custody.

## Active recall

Explain why a signature is not encryption, why password salting matters, and what a browser should validate about a server certificate.
